Camille Saint-Saëns was born in Paris in 1835. He gave his first public
concert at the Salle Pleyel in 1846, at the age of ten.

## Twenty years at the Madeleine

From 1858 to 1877 he was organist of the church of La Madeleine in
Paris, where his improvisations drew visitors. Liszt heard him there and
called him the greatest organist in the world.

The two men stayed close. When no Paris theatre wanted the opera *Samson
et Dalila*, Liszt had it staged in Weimar, in 1877. Saint-Saëns
dedicated his Third Symphony, the one with organ, to Liszt's memory in
1886.

## The piece he kept hidden

The same year he wrote *The Carnival of the Animals* for a private
party. He thought it would harm his reputation as a serious composer and
did not allow it to be published in his lifetime, with one exception:
*The Swan*.

It became his most played work after his death.

## A first for film

In 1908 he composed the music for *L'Assassinat du duc de Guise*, a
short silent film. It is one of the first scores written for a film by
an established composer.

He travelled widely in his last decades and died in Algiers in 1921.
