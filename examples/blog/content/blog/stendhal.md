Stendhal is the pen name of Henri Beyle, born in Grenoble in 1783. He
followed Napoleon's armies as an administrator, as far as Moscow in
1812, and spent the last part of his career as French consul in
Civitavecchia, near Rome. He died in Paris in 1842.

## Two novels

*Le Rouge et le Noir* came out in 1830. It follows Julien Sorel, a
carpenter's son who tries to rise in a society that has closed its doors
since the fall of Napoleon.

*La Chartreuse de Parme* followed in 1839. Stendhal dictated it in about
seven weeks. Its account of Waterloo, seen by a young man who is never
quite sure he was in the battle, became one of its most famous passages.

## The happy few

His books sold poorly. He ended the *Chartreuse* with the words "To the
happy few", in English, and said more than once that he would be read
much later.

One reader did not wait. In 1840 Balzac published a long and
enthusiastic article on the *Chartreuse*, at a time when few others had
noticed it.

## A name in medicine

Stendhal described being overwhelmed, almost to the point of fainting,
after a visit to the church of Santa Croce in Florence. The reaction of
some travellers in front of too much art is now called Stendhal
syndrome.
