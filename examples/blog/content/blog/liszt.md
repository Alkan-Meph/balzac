Franz Liszt was born in 1811 in Raiding, then in the Kingdom of Hungary.
He played in public as a child, studied in Vienna, and settled in Paris
as a teenager.

## One man, one piano

Concerts of the time mixed singers, soloists and an orchestra. Around
1840 Liszt began to give whole evenings by himself, and the word
"recital" was used for his London concerts that year. He also played
from memory and turned the piano sideways, so that the audience could
see his hands.

The effect on the public was strong enough for the poet Heinrich Heine
to give it a name: Lisztomania.

## After the tours

Liszt stopped touring in his mid-thirties and moved to Weimar in 1848.
There he conducted, composed, and wrote orchestral pieces in one
movement built on a poem or a painting, which he called symphonic poems.
His Piano Sonata in B minor dates from those years.

He also used his position for others, conducting the first performance
of Wagner's *Lohengrin* in 1850. Wagner later married his daughter
Cosima.

## The abbé

In 1865 Liszt took minor orders in Rome and was known from then on as
the Abbé Liszt. He spent his last years between Rome, Weimar and
Budapest, teaching without asking for a fee. He died in Bayreuth in
1886.

His years with Marie d'Agoult had already found their way into a novel:
Balzac drew on them for *Béatrix*.
