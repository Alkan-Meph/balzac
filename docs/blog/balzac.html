Balzac, a whole society in ninety books

2026-11-24

#literature

Honoré de Balzac was born in Tours in 1799 and died in Paris in 1850. In about twenty years of work he wrote some ninety novels and stories, which he gathered under one title: La Comédie humaine.

Characters who come back

The idea that holds the whole together arrived with Le Père Goriot, in 1835. Balzac decided that a character met in one book could walk into another. Rastignac, a penniless student in that novel, is a minister later on. Vautrin, the convict, turns up again under other names.

Read in sequence, the books stop being separate stories. They become one city, where you keep running into people you already know.

Nights, coffee and proofs

Balzac wrote at night, for hours on end, with a great deal of coffee. He then rewrote on the printer's proofs, filling the margins until the page had to be set again. His printers dreaded him, and his debts followed him all his life.

He married Ewelina Hańska, the Polish countess he had been writing to for almost twenty years, in March 1850. He died five months later.
