Dumas and the serial novel

2026-11-17

#literature

Alexandre Dumas was born in 1802 in Villers-Cotterêts, north-east of Paris. His father, Thomas-Alexandre Dumas, was born in Saint-Domingue to an enslaved woman and a French nobleman, and rose to the rank of general in the armies of the Revolution.

One year, two classics

Dumas first made his name in the theatre. The novels came later, and quickly: The Three Musketeers began to appear in a newspaper in 1844, and The Count of Monte Cristo started the same year.

Both were written to be read in episodes. That explains their shape: short chapters, a great deal of dialogue, and an ending that makes you want the next issue.

Not alone

Dumas worked with collaborators, the best known being Auguste Maquet, who drafted plots and first versions that Dumas then rewrote. The two ended up in court over money and credit.

A late honour

With his earnings he built the Château de Monte-Cristo, west of Paris, and lost it to his creditors within a few years. He died in 1870. In 2002 his remains were moved to the Panthéon in Paris.
